Key findings from 608k data points

1. Lag times are longer than the current rule of thumb. The dashboard uses ~24 h Olsobip→Kiunga. The 11-yr cross-correlation puts the peak at 29 h (OLS), 31 h (Palmer), 34 h (BIA) at hourly resolution — and 48 h when using 3-day rolling rain (which is the more physically meaningful signal because catchment saturation matters more than a single storm). Recommend the forecast tile shift to a 48-h lookahead window.

2. Combined-upstream rain is the strongest predictor. Single stations correlate at r=0.28–0.30 hourly / 0.40–0.44 daily. The mean of all 3 headwater stations at daily resolution correlates at r=0.56 at 48-h lag. Weight upstream signals equally, don't rely on any one gauge.

3. Mainstem is essentially co-moving. Kiunga→Kuambit lag = 4 h, r=0.91. Kuambit will hit shipping-critical levels within hours of Kiunga doing so. Manda (~7 days lag, r=0.66) and Obo (~7 days, r=0.60) integrate longer catchment areas.

4. Recession is 2.4× slower than the model assumes. Fitted k=0.066/day at Kiunga (half-life 10.5 days) vs previously-hardcoded k=0.16/day (half-life 4.3 days). The k=0.16 was too pessimistic. From current 1.21 m, in a completely rain-free scenario, Kiunga hits 0.6 m in ~10 days, 0.36 m in ~17 days, 0.12 m in ~35 days. Real dry periods are rarely completely rain-free.

5. Rain-to-rise sensitivity: ~0.7 m Kiunga rise per 100 mm upstream rainfall at each of BIA/OLS/Palmer (slope 0.007 m/mm across 11 years). But the 28-29 Aug event showed 4× higher sensitivity in dry-catchment conditions (0.024 m/mm at BIA alone). To lift Kiunga from ~1 m to the 6.5 m shipping trigger requires ~150 mm at BIA in wet conditions or ~400 mm in average conditions — this is roughly what the 28-Aug event delivered (164 mm at BIA), producing a 4 m+ rise 20 h after peak.

6. Current 2026 dry is severe but not without precedent. Kiunga at P1.9 for August is the third-lowest August in the 7-year record where Shipping Datum data exists (2015-16 and 2018-19 were also extreme). The 2023-24 dry didn't propagate this deep — this event is closer to the 2015-16 template.

7. Data quality flags. Aiambak (FLY19) has only 11 days of level data (Aug 2026 onwards). Manda + Kuambit have multi-year gaps in the early record. Palmer level record starts Apr 2018. Any station-specific baseline is only as good as the years available (shown in the "Yrs data" column above).

8. Operational implication. With Kiunga at P1.9 and recession half-life 10.5 days, the shipping trigger is only breachable via an upstream rain event. Watch Combined 3-station 24-h rainfall; anything >150 mm across all three will show as Kiunga rise ~48 h later. Set that as the earliest lead-indicator for shipping recovery.

Methodology how the numbers were derived

Data. 608,109 rows across 20 CSVs from VDV (Vista Data Vision), 10-minute intervals, 2015-01-01 to 2026-08-29. Consolidated to a single time-indexed dataframe, deduplicated by timestamp. Master parquet saved at DWTF/VDV/analysis/master.parquet (17 MB).

Current values. "Latest robust reading" = 7-day rolling median of daily medians — resistant to sensor spikes (a single-timestep 5-metre spike doesn't affect it). Raw last reading also computed and cross-checked; where the two diverge, the robust value is used and the raw spike flagged in current_status.json.

Lag correlations. Cross-correlation swept over lags 0-168 hours at 1-hour resolution, for each (upstream_signal, downstream_level) pair. Both hourly (24-h rolling rain) and daily (3-day rolling rain) variants computed — daily is more physically meaningful because catchment saturation dominates over single-storm timing. Best-fit lag and Pearson r at that lag reported.

Rainfall-to-rise. Linear regression: 48-h Kiunga rise (metres) = a + b × 24-h upstream rainfall (mm). Fitted per headwater station on 11 years of daily data. R² is modest (0.03–0.05) because Kiunga rise depends on antecedent conditions, catchment saturation, and all three upstream stations together — this model is a rough sanity-check, not a precision forecast.

Recession. Isolated "recession" segments where 3-day station-averaged rain sum ≤ 20 mm AND 3-day-smoothed level was falling for ≥ 5 consecutive days AND drop ≥ 2 cm. Fitted L(t) = L₀ × exp(-k·t). Reported median k, IQR, and count of segments. Kiunga had 14 valid recession segments over 11 years; the median k=0.066/day is well-founded.

Seasonal. For each mainstem station's level, computed monthly median / P10 / P90 across all recorded years, plus per-year monthly means for El Niño overlay. Percentile rank of the current reading computed within the current-month distribution.

Gap fill. None applied to the master data. Analysis uses only the actual observations. Where an entire station's data is missing for early years (e.g. Kuambit pre-2020, Aiambak pre-Aug-2026), correlations involving those years/stations return null. Gap map available in coverage.json.
